Air-Gapped Privacy Architecture

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 18, 2026 • Effective immediately

🔒

Summary for Humans

Bumblebee is built on a simple principle: what happens on your device stays on your device. We have no user accounts, no cloud databases, no analytics trackers, and zero telemetry. When you chat with someone, your messages travel directly through physical radio waves (Bluetooth Low Energy and Wi-Fi Direct) to their phone. We cannot read your chats, track your location, or sell your data—because we literally possess no servers to collect it.

01. Information We Do NOT Collect

Unlike centralized cloud messengers, Bumblebee operates in a 100% air-gapped capacity:

  • No Personal Identifiers: We do not collect names, email addresses, phone numbers, or social media logins.
  • No Device Identifiers: We do not collect IMEI, MAC addresses, Android Advertising ID (GAID), or hardware serial numbers.
  • No Telemetry or Crash Reports: The app does not include Firebase, Google Analytics, Sentry, Mixpanel, or any third-party tracking SDKs.
  • No Cloud Message Backups: Messages and media are never stored or routed through cloud servers or proxy relays.

02. Android System Permissions & Why We Need Them

Bumblebee requests only the physical hardware permissions necessary to discover nearby devices and transfer messages over local radio:

BLUETOOTH_SCAN, BLUETOOTH_ADVERTISE, BLUETOOTH_CONNECT

Required by Android to broadcast service discovery UUIDs and discover other Bumblebee devices in physical proximity.

ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION & ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION

Mandated by Android OS security policy to permit radio scanning (Bluetooth Low Energy and Wi-Fi Direct). Bumblebee uses this permission solely to discover neighboring devices. Your geographic coordinates are never recorded, monitored, or transmitted anywhere unless you explicitly tap "Send SOS".

NEARBY_WIFI_DEVICES (Android 13+) & ACCESS_WIFI_STATE

Used to establish direct ad-hoc Wi-Fi Direct connections between handsets for transferring high-resolution photos and voice memos at speeds up to 250 Mbps without an external router or internet.

RECORD_AUDIO

Used solely when you press and hold the Push-To-Talk (Walkie-Talkie) microphone button to record offline AAC voice memos. Audio is encoded locally and streamed directly to your connected peer.

POST_NOTIFICATIONS & FOREGROUND_SERVICE_CONNECTED_DEVICE

Allows the app to keep the radio listener alive in the background and notify you when a nearby peer sends you an offline message or emergency alert.

03. Emergency SOS Telemetry Disclosure

Bumblebee includes an Emergency SOS Beacon designed for search-and-rescue and disaster scenarios. When you explicitly trigger an Emergency SOS:

  • The app queries your phone's hardware GPS satellite receiver for your last known latitude, longitude, and accuracy.
  • The app queries the battery manager for your current hardware battery percentage.
  • This distress packet is broadcast over radio waves to all listening devices within range.
  • Disclosure: Because this is an emergency distress broadcast, GPS coordinates and battery levels are broadcast in plaintext over the radio so any nearby rescuer can locate you immediately.

04. Data Storage & Security

All data generated inside the app is protected by standard Android OS security:

  • Local Sandbox: Messages and peer profiles are stored in a private Room SQLite database at /data/data/com.bumblebee.chat/databases/, protected by Android Linux UID process isolation. No other app on your device can read this data.
  • UKEY2 Mutual Authentication: Peer connection sessions are authenticated using UKEY2 Elliptic-Curve Diffie-Hellman handshakes, verified visually via 4-digit Short Authentication String (SAS) matching codes.
  • Total Erasure: Navigating to Settings ➔ "Reset Session" purges all local messages and peer records immediately. Uninstalling the app permanently destroys all local databases.

05. Third-Party Services

Bumblebee communicates using Google Play Services Nearby Connections API. The Nearby Connections framework negotiates local physical radio protocols on Android. No user messages, chat contents, or voice recordings are sent to Google cloud servers.
