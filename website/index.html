Under the Hood: 3-Phase P2P Radio

How Bumblebee establishes direct high-bandwidth links without routers or cellular infrastructure.

01

Phase 1: BLE Autonomous Discovery 2.4 GHz Ultra Low Power

Devices advertise a lightweight service UUID while scanning for neighboring phones. Radios consume minimal battery until a peer is spotted.

02

Phase 2: Mutual 4-Digit PIN Handshake Curve25519 ECDH

Both users review identical 4-digit verification digits on their screens to ensure no man-in-the-middle or spoofing before accepting.

03

Phase 3: High-Speed Wi-Fi Direct Nectar Tunnel Up to 250 Mbps Local Radio

Radio negotiates a direct ad-hoc Wi-Fi tunnel. Voice memos, HD photos, and delivery receipts stream directly between radios with sub-second latency.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about off-grid mesh communication.

Does Bumblebee really work with zero internet?

Yes, 100%. Bumblebee communicates purely through physical radio waves (Bluetooth Low Energy and Wi-Fi Direct). No cell towers, SIM cards, Wi-Fi routers, or internet service providers are ever used.

What is the communication range?

Direct peer-to-peer range typically spans 30 to 100+ meters in open air or clear line-of-sight. In multi-hop mesh mode, packets hop across intermediate devices up to 7 hops away.

Why does it need Location permission on Android?

Google Android's security architecture mandates Location permission for apps performing Bluetooth scanning and Wi-Fi Direct discovery. Bumblebee uses this solely for radio device discovery and never uploads your location to any server.

How does Bumblebee send high-resolution photos offline?

Standard Google Nearby Connections has a 32 KB limit on single payloads. Bumblebee's proprietary Nectar engine slices 1280px photos into 18KB chunk packets and reassembles them smoothly on the receiving device with zero visual degradation.

Is it open-source?

Yes! Bumblebee is completely open-source under the permissive MIT License. You can review the code, build from scratch, or contribute on GitHub.
